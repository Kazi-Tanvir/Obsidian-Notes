# 3.1 The Management Spectrum — Full Note

---

## Overview

- Effective software project management focuses on the **four P's**: **People, Product, Process, and Project**
- The order is **not arbitrary** — it reflects priority

| If a manager neglects... | The consequence is... |
|---|---|
| **People** | Will never have success (SE is an intensely human endeavor) |
| **Product** (customer communication) | Risks building an elegant solution for the **wrong problem** |
| **Process** | Inserting competent methods and tools into a **vacuum** |
| **Project** (solid plan) | Jeopardizes the success of the product |

---

## 3.1.1 The People

- The "people factor" has been discussed since the **1960s**
- The **Software Engineering Institute (SEI)** developed the **People Management Capability Maturity Model (PM-CMM)**
  - Purpose: Enhance readiness of software organizations to undertake increasingly complex applications by helping to **attract, grow, motivate, deploy, and retain** talent

### PM-CMM Key Practice Areas

| # | Practice Area |
|---|---------------|
| 1 | Recruiting |
| 2 | Selection |
| 3 | Performance Management |
| 4 | Training |
| 5 | Compensation |
| 6 | Career Development |
| 7 | Organization and Work Design |
| 8 | Team / Culture Development |

- Organizations with **high maturity in people management** → higher likelihood of implementing effective SE practices
- PM-CMM is a **companion** to the Software CMM (which guides creation of a mature software process)

## 3.1.2 The Product

- Before planning, three things must be established:
  1. **Product objectives and scope**
  2. **Alternative solutions**
  3. **Technical and management constraints**

- Without this information, it is **impossible** to define:
  - Reasonable cost estimates
  - Effective risk assessment
  - Realistic task breakdown
  - Manageable project schedule

### Objectives vs. Scope

| Aspect | Definition |
|--------|------------|
| **Objectives** | Overall goals for the product *from the customer's point of view*, without considering how goals will be achieved |
| **Scope** | Identifies the primary **data, functions, and behaviors** that characterize the product; attempts to **bound** these characteristics quantitatively |

- Developer and customer must **meet** to define objectives and scope
- This begins as part of **system engineering / business process engineering** and continues into **software requirements analysis**

### Alternative Solutions

- Once objectives and scope are understood → **alternative solutions** are considered
- Alternatives help select the "best" approach given constraints:
  - Delivery deadlines
  - Budgetary restrictions
  - Personnel availability
  - Technical interfaces

## 3.1.3 The Process

- A **software process** provides the **framework** for a comprehensive development plan
- Components of the framework:

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Framework activities** | Small number; applicable to **all** software projects regardless of size or complexity |
| **Task sets** | Tasks, milestones, work products, and QA points — enable framework activities to be **adapted** to project characteristics and team requirements |
| **Umbrella activities** | Software Quality Assurance (SQA), Software Configuration Management (SCM), and Measurement — **independent** of any one framework activity; occur **throughout** the process |

---

## 3.1.4 The Project

- We conduct planned and controlled projects because it is the **only known way to manage complexity**
- **1998 industry data:**
  - **26%** of software projects **failed outright**
  - **46%** experienced **cost and schedule overruns**

- To avoid project failure, the project manager and engineers must:
  1. Avoid common **warning signs**
  2. Understand **critical success factors** for good project management
  3. Develop a **commonsense approach** for planning, monitoring, and controlling the project

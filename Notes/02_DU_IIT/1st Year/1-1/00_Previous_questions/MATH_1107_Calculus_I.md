## 📝 Final Term Exam

### 17th Batch

**Marks: 60 | Duration: 3 hours**

*Instructions: Answer any 5 (five) of the following questions. When answering a question, please answer all the subsections of it at once.*

**1.**
- **(a)** A function $f$ is defined by `[6]`
  $$f(x) = \frac{x+2}{x-1}, \quad x \neq 1.$$
  - (i) Determine the domain and range of $f$.
  - (ii) Sketch the graph of $f$, clearly showing any asymptotes and intercepts.
  - (iii) The function $g$ is obtained by transforming $f$ according to $g(x) = -2f(x-3) + 1$. Describe, in order, the transformations that map the graph of $f$ onto the graph of $g$.

- **(b)** Consider the family of quadratic functions `[6]`
  $$f_k(x) = kx^2 - 4x + 3, \quad k > 0.$$
  - (i) Determine the range of $f_k$ when $k=2$.
  - (ii) For $k=1$, find the inverse function $f_1^{-1}(x)$ and state its domain and range.
  - (iii) Discuss briefly how the graph of $f_k(x)$ changes as $k$ increases.

**2.**
- **(a)** Let `[6]`
  $$f(x) = \frac{x^3 - 3x^2 + 4}{x^2 - 4}$$
  - (i) Compute the following limits: $\lim_{x \to 2^-} f(x)$, $\lim_{x \to 2^+} f(x)$, $\lim_{x \to \infty} f(x)$
  - (ii) Using the formal $\epsilon - \delta$ definition of a limit, prove that $\lim_{x \to 1} (3x - 2) = 1$. Identify $\epsilon$ and find a suitable $\delta$ in terms of $\epsilon$.
  - (iii) The function $g(x) = e^x \sin(x)$ is continuous everywhere. Using the properties of continuous functions, explain why this is true.

- **(b)** A curve is defined parametrically by `[6]`
  $$x = t^2 - 2t, \quad y = e^t - 1, \quad t \in \mathbb{R}$$
  - (i) Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ and $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ in terms of $t$. Determine the coordinates of all points where the tangent is horizontal or vertical.
  - (ii) Find the equation of the tangent line at the point corresponding to $t = 0$.
  - (iii) Sketch the curve for $-1 \leq t \leq 3$, indicating the direction of increasing $t$.

**3.**
- **(a)** Let `[6]`
  $$f(x) = \frac{x^2 \sin x}{1 + \cos x}, \quad x \neq (2n+1)\pi$$
  - (i) Find $f'(x)$ using the quotient rule, showing all steps. Simplify as much as possible.
  - (ii) Determine the equation of the tangent line to the curve $y = f(x)$ at $x = \frac{\pi}{2}$.
  - (iii) Find all points on the curve where the tangent is horizontal.
  - (iv) A particle moves along a line with position $s(t) = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t$. Find the instantaneous velocity and acceleration. Determine when the particle is at rest, and whether it moves forward or backward immediately after that time.

- **(b)** The temperature $T$ (in °C) of a cooling object satisfies `[6]`
  $$T = 25 + 75e^{-0.1t}, \quad t \text{ in minutes.}$$
  - (i) Find $\frac{dT}{dt}$.
  - (ii) Explain the sign of $\frac{dT}{dt}$.
  - (iii) Determine the rate of change of temperature at $t=10$ min, correct to two decimal places.
  - (iv) Let $y = \cos^2(3x^2 - 2x)$. Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ and determine all $x \in [0, \pi]$ where the slope of the tangent is zero.

**4.**
- **(a)** A curve is defined implicitly by `[6]`
  $$x^2 + xy + \sin y = 4$$
  - (i) Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ in terms of $x$ and $y$.
  - (ii) Determine the equation of the tangent line at the point where $x = 1$ and $y = \frac{\pi}{2}$.
  - (iii) A point moves along this curve so that $x$ increases at a rate of $0.6 \text{ units } s^{-1}$ when $x = 1$ and $y = \frac{\pi}{2}$. Find the corresponding rate of change of $y$ with respect to time.
  - (iv) Let $g(x) = \tan^{-1}(e^{2x})$. Find $g'(x)$, and hence determine $\lim_{x \to \infty} g'(x)$. Interpret this limit in terms of the graph of $y = g(x)$.

- **(b)** A population of bacteria grows according to `[6]`
  $$P(t) = 400e^{0.25t}, \quad t \text{ in hours.}$$
  - (i) Find $\frac{dP}{dt}$ and interpret its meaning.
  - (ii) Determine how long it takes for the population to double.
  - (iii) Use differentials to approximate $\sqrt[3]{8.1}$. Compare your approximation with the exact value (rounded to four decimal places).

**5.**
- **(a)** A particle moves along a line such that its position (in meters) is given by `[6]`
  $$s(t) = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t, \quad t \text{ in seconds.}$$
  - (i) Find the velocity and acceleration functions.
  - (ii) Determine when the particle is at rest and when it is moving forward.
  - (iii) Find the total distance traveled on the interval $0 \leq t \leq 4$.

- **(b)** Let $h(x) = x^2 - 4x + 3$ on the interval $[1, 3]$. `[6]`
  - (i) Verify that $h$ satisfies the hypotheses of Rolle's Theorem and find the value of $c$ guaranteed by the theorem.
  - (ii) Using the Mean Value Theorem, find all $c$ such that $h'(c) = \frac{h(4) - h(1)}{4 - 1}$.

**6.**
- **(a)** Let $y = f(x)$ be a smooth curve on the interval $[a, b]$, and assume that $f(x) \geq 0$ for $a \leq x \leq b$. Mention why the function $f$ has a maximum value $K$ and a minimum value $k$ on $[a, b]$. If $L$ is the arc length of the curve $y = f(x)$ between $x = a$ and $x = b$, and if $S$ is the area of the surface generated by revolving this curve about the $x$-axis, then prove that `[6]`
  $$2\pi k L \leq S \leq 2\pi K L.$$

- **(b)** Use the results of part (a) above to show that the area $S$ of the surface generated by revolving the curve $y = \sec x$, $0 \leq x \leq \frac{\pi}{3}$ about the $x$-axis satisfies `[6]`
  $$\frac{2\pi^2}{3} \leq S \leq \frac{4\pi^2}{3}\sqrt{13}.$$

**7.**
- **(a)** Consider the nonlinear equation `[6]`
  $$f(x) = e^{-x} - x = 0.$$
  - (i) Transform $f(x) = 0$ into a fixed-point form $x = g(x)$ suitable for iteration, and determine an interval $[a, b]$ where $g(x)$ is a contraction mapping. Justify your answer by computing $|g'(x)|$ on the interval.
  - (ii) Starting from $x_0 = 0$, use the iteration $x_{n+1} = e^{-x_n}$ to compute the next four iterations ($x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4$) and estimate the root correct to four decimal places.
  - (iii) Show that if the iteration were instead defined as $x_{n+1} = 1 - e^{-x_n}$, the method does not converge on $[0, 1]$. Explain why, using the Fixed Point Theorem.

- **(b)** Solve the system of nonlinear equations using Newton's method: `[6]`
  $$\begin{cases} x^2 + y^2 - 4 = 0 \\ e^x + y - 1 = 0 \end{cases}$$
  - (i) Write the system in vector form $\mathbf{F}(x, y) = \mathbf{0}$ and compute the Jacobian matrix $J(x, y)$.
  - (ii) Starting from the initial guess $(x_0, y_0) = (1, 0)$, perform two iterations of Newton's method, showing all steps clearly. Use $\mathbf{X}_{n+1} = \mathbf{X}_n - J(\mathbf{X}_n)^{-1}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{X}_n)$.

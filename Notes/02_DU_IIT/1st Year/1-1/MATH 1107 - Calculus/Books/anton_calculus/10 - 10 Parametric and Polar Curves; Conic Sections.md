# CHAPTER 10: PARAMETRIC AND POLAR CURVES; CONIC SECTIONS

> Mathematical curves, such as the spirals in the center of a sunflower, can be described conveniently using ideas developed in this chapter.

In this chapter we will study alternative ways of expressing curves in the plane. We will begin by studying parametric curves: curves described in terms of component functions, including methods for finding tangent lines and arc lengths. We will then introduce polar coordinate systems, discussing tangent lines, arc length, and areas enclosed by polar curves. Finally, we will review conic sections (parabolas, ellipses, hyperbolas), study rotation of axes, and explore conic sections in polar coordinates with applications in celestial mechanics and astronomy.

---

## 10.1 PARAMETRIC EQUATIONS; TANGENT LINES AND ARC LENGTH

### PARAMETRIC EQUATIONS & ORIENTATION
* **Parametric Equations:** $x = f(t), \; y = g(t)$ ($a \le t \le b$), where $t$ is the parameter and the curve $C$ is the trajectory.
* **Orientation:** The direction of increasing parameter indicated by arrows along the curve.

#### Key Examples:
* **Unit Circle (Counterclockwise):** $x = \cos t, \; y = \sin t \quad (0 \le t \le 2\pi)$
* **Unit Circle (Clockwise):** $x = \cos(-t) = \cos t, \; y = \sin(-t) = -\sin t \quad (0 \le t \le 2\pi)$
* **Line Segment from $P(x_0, y_0)$ to $Q(x_1, y_1)$:**
  $$x = x_0 + (x_1 - x_0)t, \quad y = y_0 + (y_1 - y_0)t \quad (0 \le t \le 1)$$
* **Functions Parametrically:** $y = f(x) \implies x = t, \; y = f(t)$. Inverse $y = f^{-1}(x) \implies x = f(t), \; y = t$.

---

### TANGENT LINES & DERIVATIVES

If $\frac{dx}{dt} \neq 0$:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} = \frac{g'(t)}{f'(t)} \tag{4}$$

* **Second Derivative:**
  $$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{dy}{dx}\right] = \frac{\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{dy}{dx}\right]}{\frac{dx}{dt}} \tag{8}$$
* **Horizontal Tangent:** $\frac{dy}{dt} = 0$ and $\frac{dx}{dt} \neq 0$.
* **Vertical Tangent:** $\frac{dx}{dt} = 0$ and $\frac{dy}{dt} \neq 0$.
* **Singular Points:** $\frac{dx}{dt} = 0$ and $\frac{dy}{dt} = 0$.

---

### ARC LENGTH OF PARAMETRIC CURVES

> **10.1.1 THEOREM (Arc Length Formula for Parametric Curves)**
> If a curve $C$ given by $x = x(t), y = y(t)$ ($a \le t \le b$) is traced without retracing and derivatives are continuous:
> $$L = \int_a^b \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2}\,dt \tag{9}$$

* **Surface Area of Revolution:**
  * About $x$-axis: $S = \int_a^b 2\pi y \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2}\,dt$
  * About $y$-axis: $S = \int_a^b 2\pi x \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2}\,dt$

---

### THE CYCLOID (THE APPLE OF DISCORD)
Generated by a point on the rim of a wheel of radius $a$ rolling along the $x$-axis:
$$x = a\theta - a\sin\theta, \quad y = a - a\cos\theta \tag{10}$$
* **Arc Length of One Arch:** $L = 8a$ (discovered by Sir Christopher Wren in 1658).
* **Area under One Arch:** $A = 3\pi a^2$.
* **Brachistochrone & Tautochrone Problem:** Inverted cycloid gives fastest descent under gravity and equal descent time regardless of start position.

## 10.2 POLAR COORDINATES

### DEFINITION & CONVERSIONS
A point $P$ is represented by $(r, \theta)$, where $r$ is directed distance from the pole $O$, and $\theta$ is the angle from the polar axis.
* **Equivalent Representations:** $(r, \theta) = (r, \theta + 2n\pi) = (-r, \theta + (2n+1)\pi)$.
* **Polar to Rectangular:**
  $$x = r\cos\theta, \quad y = r\sin\theta \tag{1}$$
* **Rectangular to Polar:**
  $$r^2 = x^2 + y^2, \quad \tan\theta = \frac{y}{x} \tag{2}$$

---

### FAMILIES OF POLAR CURVES

1. **Circles:**
   * $r = a$ (centered at origin, radius $a$)
   * $r = 2a\cos\theta$ (centered at $(a, 0)$, radius $a$)
   * $r = 2a\sin\theta$ (centered at $(0, a)$, radius $a$)
2. **Rose Curves:**
   * $r = a\sin n\theta$ or $r = a\cos n\theta$ ($n$ petals if $n$ is odd, $2n$ petals if $n$ is even).
3. **Limaçons:** $r = a \pm b\cos\theta$ or $r = a \pm b\sin\theta$ ($a, b > 0$):
   * $\frac{a}{b} < 1$: Limaçon with inner loop
   * $\frac{a}{b} = 1$: Cardioid (heart-shaped)
   * $1 < \frac{a}{b} < 2$: Dimpled limaçon
   * $\frac{a}{b} \ge 2$: Convex limaçon
4. **Lemniscates:** $r^2 = a^2\cos 2\theta$ or $r^2 = a^2\sin 2\theta$ (figure-8 shape).
5. **Spirals:**
   * Archimedean spiral: $r = a\theta$
   * Logarithmic spiral: $r = a e^{b\theta}$
   * Hyperbolic spiral: $r = a/\theta$
   * Parabolic spiral: $r = a\sqrt{\theta}$
   * Lituus spiral: $r = a/\sqrt{\theta}$

---

### SYMMETRY TESTS (Theorem 10.2.1)
* **About the $x$-axis (polar axis):** Replace $\theta$ by $-\theta$.
* **About the $y$-axis (line $\theta = \pi/2$):** Replace $\theta$ by $\pi - \theta$.
* **About the origin (pole):** Replace $\theta$ by $\theta + \pi$ or $r$ by $-r$.

## 10.3 TANGENT LINES, ARC LENGTH, AND AREA FOR POLAR CURVES

### TANGENT LINES TO POLAR CURVES
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{\frac{dy}{d\theta}}{\frac{dx}{d\theta}} = \frac{r\cos\theta + \sin\theta\frac{dr}{d\theta}}{-r\sin\theta + \cos\theta\frac{dr}{d\theta}} \tag{2}$$
* **Theorem 10.3.1 (Tangents at the Origin):** If $r = f(\theta_0) = 0$ and $f'(\theta_0) \neq 0$, the line $\theta = \theta_0$ is tangent to the curve at the origin.

---

### ARC LENGTH OF POLAR CURVES

> **10.3.2 THEOREM (Polar Arc Length)**
> $$L = \int_\alpha^\beta \sqrt{[f(\theta)]^2 + [f'(\theta)]^2}\,d\theta = \int_\alpha^\beta \sqrt{r^2 + \left(\frac{dr}{d\theta}\right)^2}\,d\theta \tag{3}$$

* **Total length of cardioid $r = 1 + \cos\theta$:** $L = 8$.

---

### AREA IN POLAR COORDINATES

> **10.3.4 THEOREM (Polar Area Formula)**
> The area $A$ enclosed by $r = f(\theta)$ between rays $\theta = \alpha$ and $\theta = \beta$ is:
> $$A = \int_\alpha^\beta \frac{1}{2}[f(\theta)]^2\,d\theta = \int_\alpha^\beta \frac{1}{2}r^2\,d\theta \tag{6}$$

* **Area between two polar curves $r_1(\theta) \le r_2(\theta)$:**
  $$A = \frac{1}{2}\int_\alpha^\beta \left[(r_2(\theta))^2 - (r_1(\theta))^2\right]d\theta$$

## 10.4 CONIC SECTIONS

### GEOMETRIC DEFINITIONS
* **Parabola (10.4.1):** Set of points equidistant from focus $F$ and directrix line $D$.
* **Ellipse (10.4.2):** Set of points where sum of distances to two foci is constant $2a$ ($a > c$).
* **Hyperbola (10.4.3):** Set of points where absolute difference of distances to two foci is constant $2a$ ($c > a$).

---

### STANDARD EQUATIONS (Centered at Origin)

| Conic | Standard Equation | Focal Axis / Orientation | Focus / Foci | Directrix / Asymptotes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Parabola** | $y^2 = 4px$ | Horizontal, opens right | $(p, 0)$ | Directrix $x = -p$ |
| | $y^2 = -4px$ | Horizontal, opens left | $(-p, 0)$ | Directrix $x = p$ |
| | $x^2 = 4py$ | Vertical, opens up | $(0, p)$ | Directrix $y = -p$ |
| | $x^2 = -4py$ | Vertical, opens down | $(0, -p)$ | Directrix $y = p$ |
| **Ellipse** | $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ ($a > b$) | Major axis on $x$-axis | $(\pm c, 0), \; c^2 = a^2 - b^2$ | Vertices $(\pm a, 0)$ |
| | $\frac{x^2}{b^2} + \frac{y^2}{a^2} = 1$ ($a > b$) | Major axis on $y$-axis | $(0, \pm c), \; c^2 = a^2 - b^2$ | Vertices $(0, \pm a)$ |
| **Hyperbola** | $\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ | Focal axis on $x$-axis | $(\pm c, 0), \; c^2 = a^2 + b^2$ | Asymptotes $y = \pm \frac{b}{a}x$ |
| | $\frac{y^2}{a^2} - \frac{x^2}{b^2} = 1$ | Focal axis on $y$-axis | $(0, \pm c), \; c^2 = a^2 + b^2$ | Asymptotes $y = \pm \frac{a}{b}x$ |

---

### REFLECTION PROPERTIES & APPLICATIONS
* **Parabolas (10.4.4):** Rays parallel to axis reflect to the focus (satellite dishes, headlights, parabolic mirrors).
* **Ellipses (10.4.5):** Rays from one focus reflect to the other (whispering galleries, lithotripsy).
* **Hyperbolas (10.4.6):** Rays directed toward one focus reflect toward/away from the other (LORAN navigation, GPS).

## 10.5 ROTATION OF AXES; SECOND-DEGREE EQUATIONS

### GENERAL QUADRATIC EQUATION
$$A x^2 + B xy + C y^2 + D x + E y + F = 0 \tag{2}$$

### ROTATION FORMULAS
When axes are rotated counterclockwise by angle $\theta$:
$$x = x'\cos\theta - y'\sin\theta, \quad y = x'\sin\theta + y'\cos\theta \tag{5}$$
$$x' = x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta, \quad y' = -x\sin\theta + y\cos\theta \tag{6}$$

### ELIMINATING THE $xy$-TERM

> **10.5.1 THEOREM**
> To eliminate the cross-product term $Bxy$, choose rotation angle $\theta \in (0, \pi/2)$ such that:
> $$\cot 2\theta = \frac{A - C}{B} \tag{8}$$

* **Discriminant Classification:**
  * $B^2 - 4AC < 0$: Ellipse or circle (or degenerate point/no graph)
  * $B^2 - 4AC = 0$: Parabola (or degenerate lines/no graph)
  * $B^2 - 4AC > 0$: Hyperbola (or intersecting lines)

## 10.6 CONIC SECTIONS IN POLAR COORDINATES

### FOCUS-DIRECTRIX DEFINITION

> **10.6.1 THEOREM (Eccentricity $e$)**
> For focus $F$ and directrix line $D$, $\frac{PF}{PD} = e$:
> * Parabola: $e = 1$
> * Ellipse: $0 < e < 1 \quad (e = c/a)$
> * Hyperbola: $e > 1 \quad (e = c/a)$

---

### STANDARD POLAR EQUATIONS OF CONICS (Focus at Pole)

> **10.6.2 THEOREM**
> With focus at the origin and directrix at distance $d$:
> * Directrix $x = d$ (right of pole): $r = \frac{ed}{1 + e\cos\theta}$
> * Directrix $x = -d$ (left of pole): $r = \frac{ed}{1 - e\cos\theta}$
> * Directrix $y = d$ (above pole): $r = \frac{ed}{1 + e\sin\theta}$
> * Directrix $y = -d$ (below pole): $r = \frac{ed}{1 - e\sin\theta}$

* **Ellipse Parameters in Polar Coordinates:**
  $$a = \frac{1}{2}(r_1 + r_0), \quad c = \frac{1}{2}(r_1 - r_0), \quad b = \sqrt{r_0 r_1}$$
  $$r = \frac{a(1 - e^2)}{1 \pm e\cos\theta}$$

---

### KEPLER’S LAWS OF PLANETARY MOTION (10.6.3)
1. **Law of Orbits:** Each planet moves in an elliptical orbit with the Sun at one focus.
2. **Law of Areas:** The radius vector from Sun to planet sweeps out equal areas in equal times ($dA/dt = \text{constant}$).
3. **Law of Periods:** $T^2 = a^3$ (with $T$ in Earth years and $a$ in AU).

* **Perihelion (closest distance):** $r_0 = a(1 - e)$
* **Aphelion (farthest distance):** $r_1 = a(1 + e)$
